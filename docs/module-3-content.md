**Objective**
Understand who Eat Better serves, what promise it makes, how it reaches customers and how it keeps them.

**Customer Segments**
- Health-conscious families.
- Young professionals and students looking for better snacks.

- Marketplaces such as Amazon.

**Customer Relationships**
- Reviews and ratings, customer care by email, and a subscriber community.
- Content, offers and repeat-purchase combos.
- Transparency through kitchen visibility and the origin-story video.

**Key takeaway**
The left side of the canvas is about people: who they are, what they need and how you stay in touch.

## Chapter 3.3: Revenue Streams, Cost Structure & Profitability

**Objective**
Analyse how sales, categories and channels generate revenue, the major cost drivers, and the basics of unit economics.

**Revenue Streams**
- Direct sales on the website.
- Sales through marketplaces and quick commerce.
- Corporate gifting and bulk orders.
- Revenue by category: sweets and laddoos, namkeens, nuts and mixes, combos and hampers.

**Cost Structure** (typical for a food D2C brand; Eat Better has not published its own breakdown)
- Ingredients and production.
- Packaging.
- Logistics and delivery.
- Marketing and platform commissions.
- Team, kitchen and operating costs.

**Profitability**
Reported FY24 revenue was about Rs 14.7 crore, up around 165% from about Rs 5.5 crore in FY23. The business reported a net loss of about Rs 63 lakh for FY24, which is common for a fast-growing brand that invests in growth.
Unit economics means looking at what one order earns after the cost of making and delivering it.

**Key takeaway**
Growth and profit are different things. A brand can grow fast and still be working towards profitability.

**Think & discuss**
- Why might a quick-commerce sale earn less per pack than a website sale?
- List three ways a brand could improve profitability without raising prices.

## Chapter 3.4: Distribution Network & Logistics

**Objective**
To analyze Eat Better Co’s omnichannel go-to-market (GTM) strategy, evaluating how direct-to-consumer (D2C) fulfillment, quick-commerce dark stores, multi-channel inventory management, and specialized packaging preserve snack quality and ensure delivery speed.

**1. Go-to-Market (GTM) Channel Architecture**
A Go-to-Market (GTM) strategy defines how a company delivers its value proposition to target customers through aligned distribution and sales channels. Eat Better Co utilizes a four-pillar distribution strategy:
1. Own Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) Website: Serves as the central brand platform offering pan-India shipping. It captures high-margin orders, customer first-party data, repeat subscription orders, and custom product bundling.
2. E-Commerce Marketplaces: Platforms like Amazon and Flipkart expand market reach across Tier-1, Tier-2, and Tier-3 cities, leveraging established search intent and regional fulfillment centers.
3. Quick Commerce (Q-Commerce): Integration with fast-delivery platforms like Blinkit, Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart. By stocking inventory across urban dark stores (micro-fulfillment hubs), products are delivered to consumers within 10 to 20 minutes. Q-commerce serves as a key driver for impulse snacking and rapid volume growth.
4. Bulk & Corporate Channels: Custom made-to-order gift hampers for corporate clients, festive orders, and institutional events, enabling high-volume unit sales with predictable demand lead times.

**2. Channel Comparison Matrix**

| Channel | Fulfillment Model | Primary Delivery Speed | Core Strategic Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| Own Website (D2C) | Central Warehouse / 3PL Logistics | 2–5 Days (Pan-India) | Margin optimization, data collection & brand building |
| Marketplaces (Amazon) | Marketplace Fulfillment Centers | 1–2 Days (Express) | National reach & marketplace search visibility |
| Quick Commerce | Localized Dark Store Network | 10–20 Minutes | Instant gratification & impulse snacking market capture |
| Corporate / B2B | Direct Freight & Bulk Shipping | Scheduled / Campaign-based | High-volume unit sales & B2B social proof |

**3. Operational & Logistics Challenges**
1. Maintaining Freshness & Product Integrity: Preserving the crunch, taste, and structural integrity of clean-label snacks (free of artificial preservatives) requires multi-layer protective packaging and temperature-managed handling during long-distance transit.
2. Omnichannel Inventory Synchronization: Balancing stock allocation between central warehouses (for D2C/marketplaces) and hyper-local dark stores (for Q-commerce) to prevent stockouts while avoiding over-allocation and shelf-life expiration.
3. Managing Festive Demand Spikes: Seasonal peaks during Rakhi and Diwali cause dramatic surges in order volume. Logistics operations must scale warehousing throughput, packaging speed, and courier capacity without incurring high error rates.
4. Last-Mile Fulfillment Costs: Managing freight expenses, high shipping costs on low-average-order-value (AOV) single packs, and return-to-origin (RTO) friction in cash-on-delivery (COD) orders across pan-India routes.

**Key Takeaway**
Great snacks do not matter if they arrive late, broken, or stale. In the FMCG and healthy food ecosystem, logistics is an intrinsic part of the core product experience.
